# LifeLink — Drafting Room

This is the shared implementation contract for the current full-route replacement. The later user-supplied Victorian workshop references supersede the earlier white/gray/blue proposal. The approved direction is parchment, dark brown, copper, and brass. Do not reintroduce green, blueprint blue, oversized serif headings, or the interlocking-C mark.

## Visual world

Mood: a well-lit Victorian inventor's workshop. A clear writing sheet sits within a pale parchment frame. The character comes from the clasped-hook Reach mark, brass joins, ticket-like review edges, restrained instrument markings, and deliberate typewriter-key controls. Keep content readable and the app calm. No decorative gears scattered behind text, theatrical dark backdrops, simulated metal gradients, or dense ornament.

All app surfaces are Operate; errors and guidance are Read within the same shell. Build app screens code-first. The user's reference and palette take precedence over generic anti-slop palette warnings.

## Typography and spacing

IBM Plex Sans, actually loaded, for body, headings, controls, and wordmark. Weights 400 regular, 500 labels, 600 headings. Body 18px/28px; page title 32px/40px; section title 24px/32px; label/navigation 16px/24px; supporting metadata 14px/20px minimum. Mobile body stays 18px/28px; mobile titles 28px/36px. Do not use all-caps tracking for ordinary navigation or headers. Tabular numerals for aligned money, quantity, and time columns.

Spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48px. Desktop canvas gutter 40px; between major regions 32px; grouped rows 16–24px. Mobile gutter 20px. Button labels use the shared 16px label size, including large primary buttons; the large variant adds target height rather than changing the font. Controls are at least 48px tall, with at least 44px touch targets. Radius: 8px controls, 12px content/review panels. Use quiet borders without resting shadows. Only a floating drawer/dialog may have a subtle shadow.

## Shell and identity

- Desktop: stable 240px sidebar throughout all owner routes, including Solitaire. Same pale parchment surface, same order and labels; active item uses a neutral fill and font weight. No collapse/expand rail that shifts content between routes.
- Desktop 1440px arithmetic: 240 sidebar + 40 gutter + 1120 content + 40 gutter. For two regions: 720 + 32 + 368 = 1120. Chat may use a 720px conversation and a 368px History drawer while open; absent the drawer, retain a comfortable reading measure rather than stretching sentences.
- Navigation uses the existing owner/family roles and route set. Keep all existing destinations discoverable. Nav icons are one 20px outline set in fixed 24px slots; trailing status/action slots align.
- At 1100px and below, fold navigation into a labeled Menu button with an accessible sheet. At phone width use a 64px top bar with LifeLink, Menu, and the screen title below. Escape closes; focus stays in the open sheet and returns to its trigger. Never overlay controls on the text being read.
- Approved `/solitaire` guest exception: when signed out or the session is pending/unavailable, replace account navigation with a **Join LifeLink** rail at 1101px and above. Below 1101px, show LifeLink with **Sign up** and **Sign in** in the top bar; no private account navigation or Menu is shown. Sign up opens `/sign-in?mode=sign-up`; Sign in remains `/sign-in`. A confirmed signed-in session keeps the prior play navigation. This narrow extension changes no design tokens.
- The mark is **Reach**, matching `src/components/app/brand.tsx`: two filled hooks clasped together, taken exactly from the approved vector (Paper node **Flat logo mark** on **LifeLink — Logo mockup**). Use those paths unchanged, cropped to the viewBox `85 84 856 612`; do not redraw, re-stroke, or regularize them. The person's hook is ink and uses `--text` in the app so it follows the dark and more-contrast themes. LifeLink's hook is the approved Reach orange `#CB6527` in every theme; it is reserved for the mark and is not a UI color. `BrandMark` `size` sets the height and the width is 1.4×: 32px beside the wordmark in the sidebar, 28px in the compact phone header and assistant replies, 40px on recovery pages. App icons (`src/app/icon.svg`, `public/icons/`) place the mark on a parchment tile in ink `#1C1618` and orange. These are open hooks, not the rejected interlocking-C rings. Do not add pins, slots, outlines, or metal fills.
- Practice mode has one parchment notice above the page content: “Practice mode is on. No real messages are sent.” Its Settings link says “Turn off practice mode”. Do not repeat it in the sidebar footer or within Connections. The phone menu has a visible “Close” label as well as its icon.

## Signature interaction: the approval sheet

An inline review is the product-specific centerpiece. Show “Review this version”, a clear Practice indicator for samples, recipient/service, exact proposed content, timing/cost when applicable, and what approval will do. Use a quiet ticket edge or fine brass fasteners only on this review surface. Show a plain revision identifier such as “Version 2” where it helps establish what is approved; implementation must use the real version.

Primary wording is action-specific: “Approve practice message” for a simulated sample, or the real approved action's established verb. Secondary “Edit message” and “Cancel” remain visible. “Change the words” replaces the displayed message body with a prefilled editor in the same review sheet, retaining recipient and subject context. Save changes returns to review; Cancel restores the displayed wording. Preserve unsaved edits when a newer version arrives and offer a comparison before saving. On change, return to review. A preparation/approval/send/reply progress line is factual, not an invented percentage. A sent receipt never looks like an incoming reply. History is a right drawer; it does not replace current chat or left navigation.

## Route composition

- Today: a short appointment/reminder briefing, Your week, family messages and recent email, news, and a useful Ask entry. Keep it an overview: no “Needs you” action queue, pending-request count in the greeting or navigation, notification bell, or separate email-alert center. Flagged email previews show a compact “Possible scam” badge and “Open for details”; the whole row opens Email, where the full warning and advice are shown. Pending actions remain in their conversation/task review flows.
- Ask: calm empty state with useful prompt starters and a full-width composer; conversation keeps the current review inline; History opens from the header on the right. On phones, use a compact dock with textarea, microphone, and the primary label “Ask” in the same functional area. Keep a useful transcript visible above it; avoid a tall opening textarea. The Ask action submits a request to the assistant; exact-version approval remains a separate action.
- Task detail: identical review content as Ask, with explicit Back to conversation and a concise event trail.
- Messages: readable people/thread rows; the primary “Add a person” action uses the person-plus icon and copper styling and opens the Trusted Circle add form directly. Detail gives chronological incoming/outgoing messages and a review-before-sending composer.
- Email: list/reader hierarchy, automatic concern flag next to the affected message, actual warning-sign labels shown inside the email warning, and “What should I do?” for advice. The quiet status reads “No warning signs found. Sender not confirmed.” Full warning explanations stay in Email; Today uses only the compact preview hint. Email and summary read-aloud start with “Potential scam”, the saved warning signs, and advice to ask someone trusted before acting if unsure. There is no separate notification center. Use copper for connecting a missing mailbox and for the main safety action; keep Reply neutral on flagged emails. Empty, loading, uncertain, error, and disconnected states are distinguishable. Do not assert guaranteed safety.
- Scam check: one page heading with source choices directly beneath it. Photo input uses adjacent Choose a photo and Take a photo buttons with a short hint; show a large preview only after selection. Keep the manual paste/upload fallback; findings explain concern and give a safe next step without clicking the suspicious link. A saved result has one visible page title, followed by the assessment and a prominent “What to do now” region. “Ask someone you trust” explains the review-before-sending action before listing real contacts. Evidence, the saved original wording, and check-method details follow the actions. Keep this reading order on phones. The explanatory disclosure says “What can a scam check tell me?” rather than competing with the main action in the header.
- Trusted circle / Family: each contact shows a plain sharing summary and a labeled Permissions control that expands in place. Invitations, scope changes, and Stop sharing belong to that person; there is no separate sharing sidebar. New invitations start with no permissions selected. Read-only Family constraints are visible and revocation is understandable. Hide sample contacts in the circle and scam-help picker; retain a labeled sample contact in the circle if it has active or pending sharing, so existing access can still be revoked. Keep the original sample records for saved history.
- Groceries: usable list rows, quantities, estimates, list budgets, and copy/print controls. Shopping integration is retired; the legacy practice-shopping route explains unavailability and returns to grocery lists.
- Connections: task groups such as Messages, Email, and Calendar. Each service is one calm card (two columns on desktop, one on phones) with its name, catalog summary, neutral status label, a row of permission icons for what it can do, and a footer count with “Open”. The whole card is a single button; it holds no other controls. Keep cards sparse: setup steps, requirements, and actions live in the dialog, not on the card. Grocery shopping is not an offered connection. iMessage says “Link iMessage” or “Set up iMessage”; consent links an already registered contact. No “Agree and register”, no LifeLink provisioning/capacity charge. Missing contact recovery: add them in the Photon dashboard, then retry. The exact-address/first-message requirements remain visible in the iMessage dialog.
- Connections keeps the same order at desktop and phone widths: Message family and friends (iMessage, Discord), Read and write email (Gmail, Outlook), Manage appointments (Google Calendar), then Other services and limitations (a parchment disclosure row that reveals the same cards). Opening a card shows a service dialog, the one place a Connections shadow is allowed: group name, service title, status, a one-line intro, practice/setup/saved-real notes, “What {service} can do” (icon, capability, availability), “Not available yet”, then a parchment “What you need” box. The footer holds Cancel (outline) at bottom left and the single primary action at bottom right: “Connect {service}”, “Use a practice account” in Practice, “Try connecting again” after a failure, or “Open Trusted circle” for handle services. Once connected, Cancel reads Close and the disconnect action sits beside it on the right. Inline failures appear directly above the footer buttons. Escape and Close return focus to the card. Account states use neutral labels of at least 16px, with warning/error treatments when needed. Practice offers only local practice-account actions and preserves saved real connections. Services with no available features have no connect action, and their intro says so instead of promising capabilities.
- Isometric key icons: one set of objects resting on a round typewriter key (`src/components/app/iso-icon.tsx`), used for what a connection can do and for file types in Documents (document, PDF, spreadsheet, slides, folder, other file; pictures reuse the photo-frame key). Documents shows one beside each file row and a larger one in the preview header. One angle, one 1.4px ink line, flat faces from theme tokens only: paper/neutral/border for bodies, copper for the action detail, brass for hardware. No gradients or simulated metal. Icons are decorative (`aria-hidden`) and always sit beside words. Unavailable capabilities use the same icon at 55% opacity; a service with nothing available uses the dashed empty-key icon. Add a new icon by extending the set, not by mixing in another icon style.
- Settings: readable section rows for display, voice, notifications, profile, practice, and account; clear save/result state.
- Solitaire: persistent shell, uncluttered card table, large legible cards, keyboard/touch play, preserved offline behavior.
- Mahjong: the same public, offline, locally saved pattern as Solitaire, listed with it in a **Games** navigation group. Tiles are real Chinese characters as plain text (no Unicode mahjong symbols, no artwork) with a small corner number, wind letter, 花, or 季 so anyone can match them. Suit ink: 萬 copper, 筒 ink, 條 brass ink; 中 uses error ink and 發 uses ink, because green stays out of the palette. Free tiles are bright; blocked tiles fade to 55% and say why when tapped. Selected uses the copper ring, hints the dashed brass ring; picking one hinted tile keeps the ring on its partner. When no pair is free, Shuffle becomes the filled primary button. The board never grows taller than the screen, so the whole board and the message line fit together. Undo, Hint, Shuffle, and the Easy/Classic layout choice are large buttons in a column right of the board when the content area is at least 56rem, and below it otherwise. No letter shortcuts: only the arrow keys, Enter, and Escape, described to screen readers. On phones (under 640px wide, or under 480px tall when turned sideways; see `src/features/mahjong/screen.ts`) the route shows a “needs a bigger screen” note with **Play Solitaire instead**, and phone navigation, the sign-in link, and the offline page leave Mahjong out. No timer, score, sound, or animation.
- Sign-in/onboarding: the same mark/type/palette, short trustworthy copy, labeled inputs; onboarding explains role and practice without inventing backend capabilities.
- Not found/callback error/app error/offline fallback: plain explanation, recovery action, and route context. Offline recovery offers retry and access to Solitaire (and Mahjong on tablet-sized screens). No dead-end page.

Solitaire's and Mahjong's public server-rendered/cacheable shells remain guest-only; client `useSession` discovers signed-in state. Authentication must never gate or remount the game. The browser-only table mount resumes the local save independently of session discovery; saves remain on this device in `localStorage`.

## State and accessibility contract

Body/placeholder contrast at least 4.5:1 and large text/control indicators at least 3:1. Copper/ink carry text; brass remains hardware. Visible 2px copper focus outline with 3px offset. Explicit labels and state words accompany every color signal. Errors live beside the affected region and preserve user input. Loading keeps the final frame without shimmer. Empty views name a useful next step. Disabled buttons have visible reasons. Reduced motion removes decorative motion; functional feedback is brief (120–180ms). No entrance choreography.
